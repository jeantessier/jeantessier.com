When I started this blog, I was sharing my thoughts on
[what a software engineer is](#2003-09-12), as a producer of software, with the
goal of producing software.  I was going through various names and titles for my
profession.  I wanted to find the one that best fit me.  I was focusing on the
production of software and how the various names related to various approaches,
their expectations, and their experiences.  To a lay person, the differences can
feel like hair-splitting.  So I use _software engineer_ in general because it is
a term that is widely understood and accepted.  But in my heart, I feel that
_software <del>craftsman</del> craftsperson_ is
[a better fit for me](#2004-05-20).

Today I came upon a
[post](https://techhub.social/@datarama@hachyderm.io/117456670878858523) that
takes it a step further.  It posits:

> the practice of programming is about improving and formalizing our
> understanding of processes and systems, for the benefit of our shared humanity
> and all the life of this planet.

This moves my profession from a pragmatic "write software" to a very lofty
"benefit humanity."  Reminds me of [Marcus Aurelius](../Books.html#Meditations)
says the purpose of humans is to help each other.  For me, it also parallels
how the martial art of kendo is pragmatically about combat, but its ultimate
purpose is
["to promote peace and prosperity among all peoples."](https://www.kendo.or.jp/en/knowledge/kendo-concept/)
