A quick look promises a nice style guide for writing Ruby code.  Language
communities adopt a common style over time.  Basic formatting has broad rules,
with guidelines on when to break them.  Each topic is efficiently explained,
often with surprising depth.

I really like how Ruby's control structures discourage the use of the `NOT`
operator.  It keeps things simple for humans to read and understand.

Strings represent data and have many methods for their manipulation and
transformation.  Symbols represent concepts and the only thing they really need
is comparison.  It makes no sense to take a substring of a symbol; that would be
another, different symbol.  The author recommends using double quotes for
strings most of the time.  I have been using double quotes only when I need
interpolation.  Am I doing a bad?

The `x` suffix on regular expressions was new to me and introduced a very nice
pattern of documenting regular expressions inlined comments.  And there was an
interesting parallel between regular expressions and pattern matching: match a
string or form and pull some bits out of it.

I was surprised they didn't mention end-less methods, which were introduced
earlier.  While they showed an example with guard clauses, they didn't mention
this pattern either.

I couldn't finish the book at this time.  I am not using Ruby for work right
now, and I need to learn about AI agents.  But the next chapters delve into
larger and larger chunks of code, from methods to blocks to modules.  It even
goes into metaprogramming, gems, and DSLs.
